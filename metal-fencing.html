Why Custom

Stock Fence Panels Assume Flat Ground

Most fencing sold by the panel is designed for a level suburban lot with a straight property line. A great deal of Ventura County is neither — hillsides above Ventura and Ojai, canyon lots toward Malibu, and older properties whose lines bend around trees and retaining walls.

Fabricating to the actual run means the panels fit the grade instead of leaving triangular gaps at the bottom, and the posts land where the ground will actually hold them.

Ornamental Iron & Tubular Steel Fencing

Open picket fencing secures a boundary without walling off the view or the light. It suits estate frontages, courtyards and pool surrounds, and it is the style that pairs most naturally with a matching driveway gate.

Local Conditions

Fencing a Slope: Raked or Stepped

Sloped ground is where stock fencing falls apart and where the design decision actually matters. There are two ways to handle it.

Raked — the whole panel tilts to follow the grade, so the top rail runs parallel with the ground. It keeps a continuous line and no gaps underneath. It suits picket and ornamental designs, where angled verticals still read correctly.

Stepped — each panel stays perfectly level and drops at the posts, creating a staircase along the run. Almost always the right answer for horizontal slat designs, because raking a horizontal fence makes the lines fight the ground. The trade-off is a triangular gap under each panel, which we close with a kick panel or grading where it matters.

We walk the line before quoting, because the same fence on the same property can cost meaningfully more or less depending on which approach the ground requires.

Check Before You Build

Pool Barrier Fencing

If any part of your fence encloses a pool or spa, it is a safety barrier before it is a design choice, and California sets requirements for it.

Those cover barrier height, the gaps between vertical members, and — the one people rarely anticipate — whether the design can be climbed. A horizontal slat fence is attractive and popular, but horizontal rails at regular intervals can function as a ladder for a small child, which is exactly what a pool barrier must not do. Gates in that barrier have their own self-closing and self-latching requirements.

We establish whether your run is part of a pool barrier at the site visit and design to what applies. Our guide to California pool fence requirements covers it in more depth, and side gates covers the gate side of the same rules.

Common Questions

Metal Fencing FAQs

How much does metal fencing cost per linear foot?

The main drivers are height, style, and how difficult the ground is. A straight run of simple tubular steel on level ground is the low end; ornamental iron with hand-formed detail on a sloping hillside is the high end, because sloped ground means each panel is fabricated to its own step. Post footings in rock or hardpan also add labor. We walk the line, measure it, and give you a free written estimate rather than a per-foot guess.

Can you fence a sloped or hillside property?

Yes, and it is a lot of what we do around here. On a slope a fence is either raked, where the panel follows the grade at an angle, or stepped, where each panel stays level and drops at the posts. Stepping usually looks better with horizontal designs; raking suits pickets. Either way the panels have to be fabricated for that specific run, which is exactly why a custom shop suits hillside lots better than stock panels.

Does my fence need to meet pool code?

If it forms part of the barrier enclosing a pool or spa, yes. California sets requirements for barrier height, the gaps between vertical members, whether the design can be climbed, and the hardware on any gate in that barrier. A decorative pattern with wide openings or horizontal rails that work like a ladder can fail even when the height is right. We confirm whether your run is part of a pool barrier before designing it.

How does metal fencing hold up near the ocean?

Well, if the preparation and coating are right for the exposure. Salt air is hard on steel, so a coastal property in Oxnard, Ventura or Malibu needs a properly prepared and coated finish, and galvanizing beneath the coating is worth considering on the most exposed runs. We specify based on where your fence actually sits rather than applying one finish everywhere.

Can you match the fence to my gate?

Yes, and building them together is the best way to get it right. When a fence and driveway gate are designed as one run, the picket spacing, rail heights and finish line up so the gate reads as part of the fence rather than an addition to it. We can also match a new fence to an existing gate you want to keep.
