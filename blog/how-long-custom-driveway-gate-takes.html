How Long Does a Custom Driveway Gate Take? A Realistic Timeline

August 28, 2026 6 min read By Legendary Metal Works

"How long will it take?" is the second question everyone asks, right after price. And it is the one where vague answers cause the most trouble, because a driveway gate is not a product you take off a shelf — it is a thing that gets built.

Here is a realistic timeline for a custom gate in Ventura County, what happens in each stage, and the three things that most often stretch it.

The Short Answer

For a typical custom driveway gate with an operator: roughly four to eight weeks from signed quote to working gate. Simple gates on straightforward sites land at the short end. Ornamental gates, wide openings, coastal specifications and sites needing new electrical land at the long end.

Two things are worth saying plainly. First, most of that time is fabrication, not waiting. Second, the install itself is usually one to two days.

Stage by Stage

1. Consultation and measure — a few days

Someone comes out, measures the opening, looks at the slope, checks where power is, and talks through what you want. This is also where the swing-versus-slide question gets settled, because the driveway usually decides it rather than preference. Our sliding vs swing guide explains what to look for.

2. Design and approval — a few days to two weeks

Drawings, then revisions. This stage takes exactly as long as the decisions take. A homeowner who knows what they want approves in two days. A couple deciding between three ornamental options, or a household waiting on an HOA architectural committee, takes longer — and the HOA is genuinely outside anyone's control.

If you live somewhere with an architectural review board — common in parts of Westlake Village, Thousand Oaks, Calabasas and Malibu — start that submission early. It is the single most common reason a gate project sits still.

3. Fabrication — two to five weeks

This is the bulk of it, and it is real work: cutting and squaring the frame, fitting pickets or panels, welding, grinding every weld back, prep, primer and finish. Ornamental detail is hand labor and adds directly to this stage. So does a coastal specification, because galvanizing is an outside process with its own queue.

Powder coating is also usually an outside step, and coaters have schedules. A gate that is ready to finish on a Friday may not get coated until the middle of the following week. That is normal.

4. Site work — overlaps fabrication

Post footings get dug and poured while the gate is being built, and concrete needs time to cure properly before a heavy gate hangs off it. If power has to be run to the gate — trenching, conduit, a new circuit — this is when it happens.

Running electrical is the stage most likely to surprise a schedule, because it depends on distance, what is buried in the way, and whether the house panel has capacity.

5. Installation — one to two days

Hanging the gate, setting the operator, wiring the safety photo eyes, programming the keypad and remotes, adjusting limits and force settings, and testing. The safety setup is not a formality — an automatic gate is powered machinery and the photo eyes and force limits are what make it safe to have around children and pets.

6. Walkthrough — the same day

You get shown the manual release, how to add a remote, and what the fault indicator does. Learn where the manual release is on the day, not during a power cut in the rain.

What Actually Causes Delays

In order of how often we see it:

  • Design decisions. Not a criticism — it is your gate for the next twenty years and it is worth deciding properly. Just know that this stage is on your clock, not the shop's.
  • HOA or architectural review. Entirely outside our control and frequently the longest single stage. Submit early.
  • Electrical. If there is no power near the driveway, this is a project inside the project.
  • Outside finishing. Galvanizing and powder coating are queues we book into, not steps we control.
  • Weather. Rarely a factor here, but footings and finishing both dislike rain.

How to Make It Go Faster

  • Take photos before you call. The opening from the street, the driveway looking back at the house, and where the nearest power is. It shortens the first visit considerably.
  • Start the HOA submission the week you decide. Not after the drawings are final.
  • Settle the access question early. Who needs to get in — family, guests, deliveries, gardener, mail — determines the keypad, intercom and remote package, and it is easier to build in than to add.
  • Decide the ornamental level and stop. Changes after fabrication starts are the expensive kind of delay.

A Note on Very Fast Quotes

If someone promises a custom gate in ten days, ask what part of the process they are skipping. Usually it is either the finish system — paint over bare metal instead of proper prep, primer and powder coat — or it is a stock gate being presented as custom. Both look fine on day one. The difference shows up in year three.

Common Questions

How long does a custom driveway gate take from start to finish?

Typically four to eight weeks from signed quote to working gate. Simple gates on straightforward sites land at the short end; ornamental gates, wide openings, coastal finishes and sites that need new electrical run land at the long end. The installation itself is usually one to two days.

What takes the longest?

Fabrication is the largest block of time, but the most common cause of a project sitting still is design approval — especially where an HOA architectural committee is involved. If you have one, submit early rather than after the drawings are final.

Can the gate be installed before the electrical is run?

Sometimes, as a manual gate first with the operator added later. It is not usually the cheaper path, because it means a second mobilisation, but it is an option when the electrical work is holding everything else up.

Do I need a permit for a driveway gate?

It depends on your city or county jurisdiction and on the specifics of the gate. We check that as part of the quote rather than leaving it to you, and where a permit is required we pull it — that is part of holding a contractor licence.

Is a ten-day custom gate realistic?

Rarely, and it is worth asking what is being skipped. Usually it is either the finish system — paint over bare metal instead of prep, primer and powder coat — or a stock gate presented as custom. Both look fine on day one.
