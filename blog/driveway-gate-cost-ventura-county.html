How Much Does a Driveway Gate Cost in Ventura County? 2026 Prices

August 28, 2026 8 min read By Legendary Metal Works

This is the first question almost everyone asks, and most websites answer it with "it depends." That is true, but it is not useful. Here is what a driveway gate actually costs in Ventura County in 2026, what moves the number, and where people most often get surprised.

One thing to say up front: these are installed ranges for real projects in this county, not material-only prices. A quote that looks dramatically cheaper than the bottom of these ranges is usually leaving something out — most often the electrical, the post footings, or the safety devices.

The Short Answer

  • Basic single swing gate, steel, simple design: $3,000 – $6,000 installed
  • Double swing gate, ornamental iron: $6,000 – $15,000 installed
  • Sliding or rolling gate, steel or iron: $5,000 – $12,000 installed
  • High-end custom estate gate: $15,000 – $40,000+ installed

Those ranges hold for most of Ventura County. They shift upward in Malibu, on steep hillside lots, and anywhere the gate has to be built to a coastal specification.

What Actually Drives the Price

1. The width of the opening

This is the single biggest factor, and it is not linear. A 12-foot opening and a 20-foot opening are not 60% apart in cost — they are further apart than that, because a wider gate needs heavier stock, deeper footings, and often a different operator class to move the extra weight without tearing itself apart. If your opening is wide, ask about splitting it into a double swing before assuming a single leaf.

2. Swing versus slide

Neither is universally cheaper. A swing gate is simpler to build but needs level ground and clear space to open into. A sliding gate needs a run-back area alongside the driveway, plus either a ground track or a cantilever assembly. On a sloped driveway — common in Ojai, Thousand Oaks and the hillside parts of Ventura — slope frequently rules out swing and the decision is made for you. Our sliding vs swing comparison walks through how to tell which your driveway can take.

3. Material and finish

Steel and wrought iron are the workhorses. Aluminum costs less to move and does not rust, but it dents and it does not carry the same presence. The finish matters more than people expect: a powder coat over properly prepped and primed steel is a different product from paint on bare metal, and the price difference shows up again in year seven rather than in the quote.

Near the coast — Oxnard, Ventura, Malibu — a marine-grade specification adds roughly 10 to 20 percent and roughly doubles the working life. That is not an upsell; salt air is genuinely harder on metal than most people expect. Our material comparison guide goes deeper on the trade-offs.

4. Ornamental work

Scrollwork, custom pickets, laser-cut panels and forged detail are all hand labor. This is the part of the budget you control most directly. A structurally identical gate can vary by several thousand dollars purely on how much detail is in it.

5. The operator and access control

The gate is the half people picture. The other half is the mechanism. A residential operator, safety loops, photo eyes, a keypad, a remote or two, and a way for the mail carrier and your guests to get in — that package usually runs $2,000 to $6,000 depending on the operator class and how much access control you want. Smartphone control and video intercoms sit at the top of that range.

6. The part nobody quotes: electrical and civil work

A motorized gate needs power at the gate. If there is no circuit near the driveway, trenching and running conduit is real work with real cost, and it can add anywhere from a few hundred dollars to a few thousand depending on distance and what is in the way. Post footings are the same story — a gate hangs off two points and those points have to be set in concrete deep enough to hold a heavy swinging load for decades.

When a quote looks unusually low, this is almost always what has been left out of it.

A Realistic Mid-Range Example

A double swing ornamental iron gate on a 14-foot opening, moderate scrollwork, powder-coated, with a residential operator, photo eyes, a keypad and two remotes, on a lot that already has power near the driveway, will typically land somewhere between $9,000 and $14,000 installed in Ventura County. Add a long conduit run or a hillside footing situation and that moves up.

Where People Regret Saving Money

Three places, consistently:

  • Undersized frame stock. A gate built too light for its span sags, and no amount of re-hanging fixes a frame that is deflecting. This is the failure we get called out to most often.
  • Shallow footings. Cheap to skip, expensive to correct, because correcting it means taking the posts out.
  • An operator sized for a lighter gate. It will work at first. It will not work in year three.

If a gate is already installed and giving you trouble, our guide on whether to repair or replace covers which faults are worth fixing.

How to Compare Quotes Fairly

Ask every bidder the same four questions and the pricing becomes readable:

  • What gauge or wall thickness is the frame, and what is the span?
  • How deep are the post footings, and is that included?
  • Is running power to the gate in the quote, or excluded?
  • Which operator, and what gate weight is it rated for?

Two quotes that look $4,000 apart often turn out to be the same price once all four answers are on the table.

Common Questions

Why do driveway gate quotes vary so much?

Usually because they are not quoting the same scope. The three items that most often differ are the frame stock thickness, whether post footings are included, and whether running electrical power to the gate is in the price or excluded. Ask all three bidders those questions and the spread usually collapses.

Does a motorized gate cost much more than a manual one?

The automation package — operator, safety photo eyes, keypad and remotes — typically adds $2,000 to $6,000 depending on the operator class and how much access control you want. If there is no power near the driveway, trenching and conduit is an additional line item that can range from a few hundred dollars to a few thousand.

Is a sliding gate cheaper than a swing gate?

Not reliably. A swing gate is simpler to build but needs level ground and room to open into. A sliding gate needs run-back space alongside the driveway plus a track or cantilever assembly. On a sloped driveway the slope usually makes the decision before cost does.

Do coastal properties really cost more?

Yes, and it is worth it near the water. A marine specification — hot-dipped galvanizing, zinc-rich primer, marine powder coat, stainless hardware throughout — adds roughly 10 to 20 percent and roughly doubles the service life. In Oxnard, Ventura and Malibu, skipping it is usually a false saving.

Can you work to a fixed budget?

Usually yes. Ornamental detail is the most controllable part of the price, and the structure does not have to suffer for a simpler design. Tell us the number you are working with and we will show you what it buys rather than quoting something you did not ask for.
